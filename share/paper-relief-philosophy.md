# Paper Relief

A visual philosophy of light caught between layers.

Every image is built as a stack of cut sheets. Nothing is painted; everything is *placed*. A form exists only because a
blade traced its edge and a hand lifted it a few millimetres above the sheet beneath. Depth is not rendered but
earned: each layer casts a soft, low shadow onto the next, and the eye reads the picture the way fingertips would read
a relief. The work must feel meticulously crafted, as if a master paper artist spent countless hours scoring, curling
and setting every piece by hand.

Space is architectural. A heavy outer field frames a lighter inner window, usually an arch, so the composition breathes
from the centre outward. The figure stands in the threshold between the two, breaking the frame at its base, so the
boundary between world and ornament dissolves. Negative space is not empty; it is the pale sheet that holds the light.

Colour is limited and deliberate: one deep ground tone, one luminous paper tone, one warm accent and a thread of gold
that appears only where light would catch a raised edge. Hues never blend smoothly across a form; they change at
the cut, sheet by sheet, the way coloured stock is chosen and stacked. Restraint in the palette is what lets the
layering sing, and it is calibrated with painstaking care.

Rhythm comes from repetition at many scales: petals repeated around a centre, scallops repeated along a hem, curls
repeated along a frame, stars repeated across a sky. Each repetition is slightly varied, never mechanical, the mark
of patient handwork rather than a stamp. Movement is carried by long, unbroken curves (hair, ribbon, hem) that sweep
the eye through the stack of layers.

Hierarchy is set by height. The face sits on the highest, cleanest layer with the sharpest edge; ornament recedes into
lower layers and softer shadows. Text, if any, is a whisper engraved into the margin. The finished piece should read as
master-level execution: crisp edges, consistent light direction, shadows that never contradict one another, and not a
single element out of place.
